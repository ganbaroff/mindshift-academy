# The decisions we are unsure about

Answer these in prose before you build anything. A disagreement here is worth more to us than
a beautiful mockup that assumes we were right.

## 1. The frame

We picked "mission control": child is an operator, monster is a robot on a mission, the map is a
mission board. Two frames we rejected:

- **Workshop** — every task is a part of the monster you are assembling. Progress is visible in
  the monster's body, not in numbers. Quieter, better for an anxious child.
- **Duel** — the screen splits: your command on the left, what the monster understood on the
  right. The score is how many times it misunderstood you; the goal is zero. Failure becomes
  funny instead of shameful.

Which would you pick for an 8-year-old and a 13-year-old sharing one product, and why? Is one
frame even possible across that age range, or do we need the frame to change with age?

## 2. The three-field brief

Цель / Что дано / Готово, когда — three labelled rows before the child touches anything.

Our fear: it is three lines of reading before the fun, and a child skips it. Our alternative
was folding "готово, когда" into the check button's own label, or revealing it only after the
first failed attempt. What do you think, and how would you make a child actually read it?

## 3. The re-ask

The monster asks one clarifying question instead of marking the answer wrong.

- Should the re-ask replace the feedback area, or appear as a new message under it?
- Should the child's original text stay editable, or should they retype?
- After two re-asks the monster gives its hint free. Is two right?
- How do we make it unmistakably *not* a failure — colour alone will not carry that.

## 4. The map

Fifteen dots, five clusters, one per week.

Our fear: a map that shows twelve locked dots tells a child how far they have not gone. Is there
a shape that shows progress without showing the size of what remains? Should the map only ever
reveal the current cluster?

## 5. Ranks vs a growing monster

We proposed five ranks, one per completed week (Стажёр, Оператор, Связист, Штурман, Командир).
The alternative is no ranks at all: the monster visibly grows or gains a part each week.

Which reads better to a child who cannot yet feel proud of an abstract word? Note we cannot use
social comparison of any kind — no leaderboards, no friends, no sharing — so the reward has to
land entirely inside the child's own screen.

## 6. The stuck child

The hardest case: a careful child fails twice and quits. Nothing today notices. What is the
right intervention, and how do we do it without the child feeling watched or pitied?

## 7. What we did not ask

Tell us what is missing from this brief. Especially: what does a tired child do at 8pm on a
phone with 11% battery, and what does this design do to them?
