# Asset provenance — shipped media

> Started 2026-09-06. Every binary we ship to a family belongs here with its origin and licence.
> An asset with no row is an unanswered question, and app stores, investors and counsel all ask
> it eventually.

## Audio — `public/sounds/` (10 files)

`achievement.wav`, `ambient.wav`, `click.wav`, `crystal.wav`, `fail.wav`, `gacha.wav`,
`hint.wav`, `level_up.wav`, `success.wav`, `tick.wav`.

**Origin: AI-generated. Licence: UNRECORDED.** All ten landed in one commit on 2026-06-26
(`2101360`). The founder states they were generated by the AI agent building the product at the
time; the generating tool and its terms are not recorded and cannot be reconstructed from the
repository.

## Animation — `public/lottie/` (4 files)

`celebrating.json`, `happy.json`, `sad.json`, `thinking.json` — the monster's states, loaded by
`src/components/companion/MonsterAvatarInner.tsx`.

**Origin: UNVERIFIED. Licence: UNRECORDED.** Same question as the audio and not yet answered.
Lottie JSON is a vector export, so a file taken from a stock library carries that library's
licence with it and looks identical to one drawn in-house.

Note for design, not law: `sad.json` ships but is never selected. The session page moves the
monster to `thinking` on a wrong answer, never to `sad` — a deliberate choice not to make a
child read their mistake on the companion's face. The asset is dead weight unless that changes.

## What we do know

- **Monster art** — `src/components/companion/MonsterSVG.tsx`: drawn in-repo as code, no asset file.
- **Icons** — `lucide-react`, ISC licence (verified in `node_modules/lucide-react/package.json`).
- **Fonts** — Comfortaa, Nunito and Geist Mono, served via `next/font/google`
  (`src/app/layout.tsx:2`). All three are SIL Open Font Licence 1.1.

## Disposition

Low risk, not zero, and cheap to close for the audio: [Kenney](https://kenney.nl) publishes UI
and impact sound packs under CC0 1.0 — no attribution, commercial use explicit. `sound-engine.ts`
resolves files by name, so the filenames can stay and the swap is contained.

The Lottie files are the sharper question, because a stock-library original is plausible there in
a way it is not for a synthesised beep. Establish where those four came from before public launch.

Until then, this file is the honest answer to "where did your media come from".
