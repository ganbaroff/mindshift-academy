# AI-literacy standards coverage map — MindShift Academy curriculum (2026-09-05)

Code evidence: `src/lib/evolution.ts` (`WEEK_CONCEPT`) + `titleRu`/`explanationRu` of each week's `session-1.ts` under `src/content/curriculum/week-N/`. Standards evidence: WebSearch summaries of AI4K12, UNESCO, ISTE, CSTA, EU DigComp 2.2, Finland/UK; two WebFetch attempts (AI4K12 grade-band page, CSTA interactive standards page) returned only navigation shells, no grade-specific text — flagged in §6.

## 1. What each week teaches (from code)

- **Week 1 — "precision."** Title: «Монстр слышит только то, что сказано» ("The monster hears only what's said"). Explanation: the monster never guesses — it does exactly what was named, cell by cell; a missing detail leaves the picture incomplete. Task family: complete, unambiguous, ordered natural-language instructions (grid-drawing).
- **Week 2 — "decomposition."** Title: «Сначала — потом» ("First — then"). Explanation: step order changes the outcome — say "spread butter" before "take the knife" and the monster stalls, because it executes the list literally with no inference. Task family: breaking a task into an ordered sequence of atomic steps (sandwich-making).
- **Week 3 — "conditions."** Title: «Если — то» ("If — then"). Explanation: a step-list that works on one map crashes into a wall on another; a forward-looking IF-THEN rule is needed. Task family: conditional/branching rules that generalize across situations.
- **Week 4 — "pattern."** Title: «Барабан монстра» ("The monster's drum"). Explanation: the monster drums until told "stop" — three beats you listed end at three, but a repeat-rule plays as long as asked; this week the monster gains a self-repeating back pattern. Task family: loops/repeat-rules vs. fixed enumeration.
- **Week 5 — "verification."** Title: «Монстр уверен. А ты?» ("The monster is sure. Are you?"). Explanation: a confident tone isn't truth — design a check, mark each claim true or false. Task family: verifying confident-sounding AI output rather than trusting it (this week is also the capstone, `w5-s3`).

## 3. Six biggest gaps, ranked, with placement

1. **AI learns from data (AI4K12 Big Idea 3 / UNESCO AI techniques).** Highest severity: the entire course teaches "instruct a rule-follower" and never shows the categorical difference between that and a model that learns from examples — the core idea that makes something "AI" rather than "a program." *Placement:* new session, week 3 (after conditionals, before patterns). *Task idea:* show 10 pictures already sorted into two piles by a secret rule; the kid guesses the rule, then sees how a "guessing machine" would guess differently from a fixed IF-THEN rule.
2. **Ethics/safety/bias (UNESCO Ethics of AI, DigComp 2.2 bias/discrimination, ISTE ethics guide).** No lesson content on fairness or safe data practices with AI (separate from the app's own COPPA consent, which is infrastructure, not curriculum). *Placement:* extend week 5 (verification is the natural home). *Task idea:* the monster sorts six kids' drawings into "good"/"needs work" — spot whether it's using a fair rule or an unfair one (e.g., only liking blue crayons).
3. **Societal impact / real-world AI awareness (AI4K12 Big Idea 5).** Kids never connect monster-prompting skill to voice assistants, recommendation feeds, or real chatbots they already use. *Placement:* capstone session `w5-s3` (already the graduation session per `CAPSTONE_SESSION_ID`). *Task idea:* name three places AI already talks to people in real life, and one rule the kid would give it.
4. **Loops as a named CS concept, not just "pattern."** Week 4's drum/rhythm framing may blur count-controlled repeat ("beat 5 times") with condition-controlled repeat ("beat until I say stop") — CSTA treats these as distinct. *Placement:* week 4, session 2. *Task idea:* teach the monster two kinds of repeat side by side — a counted drum roll and a roll that only stops on command — one line each.
5. **Debugging scaffolded earlier, not just week 4.** Real debugging tasks exist (per the readability audit) but only surface in `week-4/session-3`; weeks 1-3 never ask the kid to find their own mistake in a sequence or rule, only to write one correctly the first time. *Placement:* week 2 or 3. *Task idea:* show a broken "first-then" list where the monster gets stuck; the kid finds the missing step.
6. **Human-AI collaboration framing (UNESCO human-centred mindset, AI4K12 Natural Interaction).** The relationship is always commander → monster; the course never models iterative collaboration (AI proposes, human refines) as opposed to pure top-down instruction. *Placement:* week 2 or 5. *Task idea:* the monster gives its own first-guess sequence; the kid edits only the wrong step instead of rewriting everything from scratch.

## 4. Honest note: "prompting" is narrower than "AI literacy"

The five-week arc (precision → decomposition → conditions → pattern → verification) is a genuinely well-built **algorithmic-thinking / instruction-design curriculum for a rule-following, non-learning agent** — closer to "teach a robot with natural language" than to AI4K12- or UNESCO-scope "AI literacy." It is silent on: how a real AI model works (training on data, statistical prediction, hallucination as a mechanism, not just "confident tone"), bias and fairness, privacy/data practice with AI tools, environmental/societal cost, and collaborative (vs. purely directive) human-AI interaction. Week 5's "verification" is the one genuine bridge toward critical AI literacy, but it is scoped to catching a puzzle character's claims, not real-world skepticism toward deepfakes, biased recommendations, or chatbot manipulation. Given `AGENTS.md`'s own framing (a prompt-engineering course, ages 8-11, V1 cohort), this narrowness may be an intentional, defensible product-scope decision — but it should be labeled honestly as **"prompt-engineering readiness"** rather than marketed as full AI literacy against UNESCO/AI4K12 benchmarks.
